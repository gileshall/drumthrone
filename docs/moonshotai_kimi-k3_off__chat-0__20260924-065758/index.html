Rhythm and complexity

Time between strokes

Peaks line up with the subdivisions the drummer uses. Median 126 ms, entropy 2.02 bits, nPVI 23.3 (0 would be perfectly even spacing).

Position in the beat

Where in the beat each drum lands, one full turn per beat, with 16th positions at the quarters and triplets at the thirds.

Subdivisions

Each hit snapped to the nearest of 12 positions per tracked beat. Grid lock 0.07 (1 = every hit exactly on a 16th or triplet position, 0 = no relation to the grid). The lower the grid lock, the less these shares mean.

Stroke evenness

Inside steady runs (76 strokes), each stroke is compared with the midpoint of the strokes two either side, so swing counts as feel, not error. This ignores tempo drift entirely. Timing jitter 7.5 ms (4% of the stroke spacing). Measurement error was 1.6 ms on clean audio and is likely higher on a live recording.

Rhythmic vocabulary

Each beat reduced to a 12-step pattern. 27 distinct rhythms across 39 beats (29% of beats reuse an earlier one); 35 distinct once the choice of drum counts. Lempel-Ziv complexity 0.65 of a shuffled version of the same hits (1.0 = no structure).

Timing error

Median error 3.1 ms from the transcription alone, 0.8 ms after snapping each stroke to the sharpest nearby onset in the audio (89% within 5 ms). Every timing chart on this page uses refined times.

Does audio level track how hard a drum was hit?

The transcriber outputs every note at velocity 100, so dynamics come from the audio, from the peak energy in the instrument's frequency band right after each hit. Families with rho below 0.7 are left out of the dynamics charts above.

Which drums can the transcriber name?

Generated solos can use all 47 GM percussion notes, but the transcriber names fewer of them (about 20 in a typical solo), so the instrument range of a generated solo comes from its own MIDI.

Does the transcription follow the audio?

Transcribed density next to a transcription-free measure (mean spectral flux), per 2 s window. Correlation 0.84.
