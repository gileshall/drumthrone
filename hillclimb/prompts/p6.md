Using Python 3 and mido (no other MIDI libraries), write a single script, drum_solo.py, that generates the best two minute drum solo you can as a General MIDI file, solo.mid, on MIDI channel 10. It will be played back on a General MIDI synthesizer.

Make it sound like a great drummer playing:
- Develop motifs: state them, vary them, bring them back, and contrast density and dynamics to build tension and release.
- Let it flow the way a great drummer's hands do: long runs of singles and doubles that travel around the kit, rolls that swell and resolve, fills that carry into the next downbeat.
- Pace it like a showcase solo: keep the energy alive all the way through and land the final hit instead of fading away.
- Shape dynamics inside phrases, with accents, ghost notes and crescendos, more than by making whole sections louder or softer.
- Play it the way a person performs it in real time: the pulse surges and settles with the music, phrases rush ahead or hold back, and notes land where a drummer's hands put them rather than on a quantized grid, with ghost notes against accents.
- Keep inventing: a great soloist rarely plays the same bar twice, even while the motifs stay recognizable.
- Keep it playable by one drummer: at most two hands and two feet striking at any instant.
- The drum kit carries the solo; the rest of the General MIDI percussion set (notes 35–81) is there for color when it serves the music.

Running the script in an empty directory must write solo.mid there, identical on every run. Share drum_solo.py.
